v4.15.0 2026 minor
  • 🇭🇺 Hungary added — Hungarian/English toggle; 57% farmland, 21.1% forest (a century-long record as of end-2024), and one of the highest solar shares of electricity generation in the world (24% in 2024, over three-quarters of green electricity). The disclaimer notes Hungary's April 2026 election, in which Péter Magyar's Tisza party defeated Viktor Orbán's Fidesz after 16 years in power; the new government has since dropped Hungary's EU veto on Ukraine's accession and moved to restore judicial independence. Energy figures reflect the system inherited from the outgoing government, as the new administration's policy was still forming at time of writing.
  • 🇹🇭 Thailand upgraded to bilingual — added a Thai/English toggle to the previously English-only Thailand game, following the "always add the local language, or explain why not" principle now applied across the game.
v4.14.0 2026 minor
  • 🇹🇭 Thailand added — English only; 41% farmland (a top global rice exporter), 37% forest despite decades of development, and over 250 golf courses making it one of Asia's top golf tourism destinations. Highlights EGAT's floating solar programme on Sirindhorn Dam — the world's largest hydro-floating solar hybrid — which sidesteps land-use conflicts entirely by sharing reservoir surfaces already dammed for hydropower. World card: Thailand's tropical solar resource and reasonably large land area mean the world's electricity could theoretically be powered from just 33% of Thailand's land — one of the more physically grounded "fits within the country" cases in the game.
v4.13.1 2026 patch
  • Tuvalu: EEZ note added — the world card footer now addresses whether Tuvalu's vast 749,790 km² ocean territory (nearly 29,000× its land area) could host offshore solar or wind. Short answer: not easily — open-ocean floating solar has no proven cyclone-resistant design anywhere in the world, and Tuvalu's atolls drop into deep water almost immediately offshore, ruling out fixed-bottom turbines. The EEZ's real value to Tuvalu remains tuna fishing licence fees, not energy.
v4.13.0 2026 minor
  • 🇹🇻 Tuvalu added — English only; one of the four smallest countries on Earth at just 26 km² (2,600 ha), averaging under 2 metres above sea level. Categories highlight the existential threat of sea-level rise for low-lying Pacific nations: pulaka swamp-taro gardens poisoned by saltwater intrusion from below, a freshwater lens contaminated by encroaching seawater, and engineered land built by the Tuvalu Coastal Adaptation Project because there is no higher ground to retreat to. World card: Tuvalu's entire national territory would fit inside the land needed for world solar over 6,400 times — the starkest scale mismatch in the game, underlining just how small and vulnerable this nation of 11,000 people truly is.
v4.12.0 2026 minor
  • 🇳🇬 Nigeria added — English only; Africa's most populous nation (220 million) and largest oil producer, yet generating less grid electricity than Norway. 46% farmland, 22% forest (rapidly declining — one of the world's highest deforestation rates), 25% savanna. Golf courses cover more land than all utility-scale solar farms combined. World card: Nigeria's exceptional solar resources (Sokoto gets 7.5 peak sun hours/day) mean the world's electricity could theoretically be powered from 18% of Nigeria's land — roughly the same area Nigeria already uses for cities and roads.
v4.11.0 2026 minor
  • 🇬🇱 Greenland added — English only; 79% of the world's largest island is buried under the second-largest ice sheet on Earth, reaching 3 km thick. The remaining coastal fringe is tundra, bare rock and sheep pastures. The world card is the most striking in the game: the solar circle technically fits within Greenland's outline at 17.2% of total land — but represents 91% of all ice-free land on the island, essentially the entire exposed coastline. Greenland already generates 87% of its electricity from hydropower (Nukissiorfiit), using glacial meltwater from the retreating ice sheet.
v4.10.2 2026 patch
  • Russia: Settlement & roads added — cities, towns and infrastructure were missing as a separate category, buried inside "Other land". Now split out at 1.5% (~25.6M ha), covering Moscow, St. Petersburg and Russia's 13 other million-plus cities. "Other land" corrected to 32.8%.
  • Russia: land use figures corrected — categories now sum to 100% using FAO FAOSTAT 2022 territorial area (1,709.8M ha) as denominator: Forest 49.8%, Other 32.8%, Agricultural 13.0%, Water 2.9%, Settlement 1.5%.
v4.10.1 2026 patch
  • Russia: golf courses added as a category — ~35 clubs × ~65 ha ≈ 1,700 ha = 0.0001% of Russia's land. Strikingly, this is roughly the same area as all of Russia's solar farms combined, making it the most vivid illustration in the game of how little solar Russia has deployed.
  • Pakistan: "spot on!" scoring fixed — the spot-on threshold was a fixed 0.3% absolute for all non-solar categories, enormous relative to a 0.0008% answer. Now uses cat.step × 0.5 so the threshold scales with the category's resolution. (Fix was in v4.10.0; this patch confirms the correct file is in the zip.)
v4.10.0 2026 minor
  • 🇷🇺 Russia added — Russian/English toggle; nearly half of the world's largest country is boreal forest — bigger than the entire Amazon. Russia holds ~20% of global forest cover and the world's greatest freshwater reserves, yet generates just 0.28% of its electricity from solar (IRENA 2023), relying instead on gas (~47%), nuclear (~20%) and hydro (~18%). World card: despite minimal current solar, the southern steppes could power the whole world with just 1.5% of Russia's land.
v4.9.0 2026 minor
  • 🇵🇰 Pakistan added — Urdu/English toggle; ~47% farmland, just 4.7% forest, with the Thar and Cholistan deserts covering much of the rest. The solar category highlights Pakistan's extraordinary rooftop solar boom: in 2024 alone it imported more solar panels than almost any country bar China and the US — nearly all onto existing rooftops, adding essentially zero new land use. World card: Pakistan's deserts could power the whole world from just ~23% of its own land.
