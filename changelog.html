Changelog

How is land actually used? — version history · major = architectural changes · minor = new countries or significant features · patch = bug fixes and small tweaks

v5.2.0 2026 minor
  • Qatar added - Arabic/English toggle; nearly 80% desert and barren land, with over 99% of the population living in the urban fringe around Doha. The country context box directly addresses whether Qatar is OPEC's fastest-transitioning nation on renewables - it isn't: Saudi Arabia and the UAE lead the Gulf's pace of change, while Qatar has taken a more measured approach, instead expanding LNG production capacity by 85% by 2030. World card: despite near-uniform, world-class solar conditions (~375 ha/TWh), Qatar's tiny land area means the circle needed for world electricity demand is over 1,000× the size of the entire country - the most dramatic overflow case in the game, illustrating that per-hectare solar potential and total land area are entirely separate questions.
v5.1.4 2026 minor
  • Locator ring is now clickable - clicking anywhere inside the shrinking ring for a tiny country (Lebanon, Tuvalu) now loads that country's game, using the same proximity detection that triggers the ring's hover animation. This matters most for Tuvalu, whose shape is too small to click directly (and may not even exist in the underlying map data) - the ring is effectively its only clickable target on the map. A pointer cursor appears while hovering in range as a visual cue.
v5.1.3 2026 patch
  • Fixed Tuvalu's locator ring position - it was appearing in the wrong place (northwest of Australia) and not triggering at all from the dropdown. Root cause: Natural Earth's 50m dataset, used for the world map, likely omits Tuvalu entirely, so there was no map feature to compute a location from. Tuvalu now uses a hardcoded real-world coordinate instead of relying on the (probably absent) map feature, matching the location used inside Tuvalu's own game page.
  • Fixed map cropping in Chrome - the world map's top and bottom were being clipped in Chrome but not Firefox. The map used SVG "slice" mode, which crops content to fill the frame; a leftover, invalid CSS rule meant to control this had no actual effect in any browser, masking the real cause. Switched to "meet" mode, which always shows the full map without cropping, in every browser.
v5.1.2 2026 minor
  • Locator ring for tiny countries - countries too small to reliably see or click on the map (currently just Tuvalu) now get a shrinking accent-coloured ring that pinpoints their location. It triggers two ways: hovering the country's entry in the dropdown list, or simply moving the mouse near its real position on the map, even though the country's actual rendered shape is too small to hit directly.
  • Dropdown menu made more translucent again - opacity lowered from 62% to 50%, blur reduced from 9px to 7px.
v5.1.1 2026 patch
  • Country context split into its own boxout - the disclaimer box previously mixed the generic "thought experiment, not a policy recommendation" boilerplate with each country's specific facts, sources, and political context, making the specific content easy to miss. All 24 games now show a separate amber "Country context" box above the plain grey disclaimer, so country-specific notes (energy policy, political events, data sources) get their own visual space.
v5.1.0 2026 minor
  • Oman added - Arabic/English toggle; over four-fifths desert and gravel plains, with agriculture and settlement confined to a thin coastal fringe watered by the millennium-old falaj irrigation system. The disclaimer covers Oman's oil-dependent economy (75% of government revenue from hydrocarbons) alongside its Vision 2040 pivot to green hydrogen exports. World card: Oman's exceptional solar irradiance (~429 ha/TWh, among the best on Earth) means the whole world's electricity could theoretically come from 43% of Oman's land - yet independent analysis shows Oman's own 2030 green hydrogen targets alone would need more renewable electricity than its entire current grid produces, illustrating that the barrier is built infrastructure, not sunlight or land.
v5.0.3 2026 patch
  • Dropdown menu made more translucent again - opacity lowered from 72% to 62%, blur slightly reduced from 10px to 9px, for an even clearer view of the map underneath.
v5.0.2 2026 patch
  • Fixed stuck map highlight - hovering a country in the dropdown could occasionally leave a previous country highlighted (e.g. Australia staying lit after moving on to Egypt), most likely from a missed mouseleave event during fast mouse movement, or a leftover selection state sharing the same highlight colour. Made the highlight self-healing: every hover now clears all highlights before applying the new one, with an additional safety net that clears everything if the pointer leaves the dropdown entirely.
v5.0.1 2026 patch
  • Dropdown menu made more translucent - background opacity lowered from 98% to 72%, with a stronger blur to compensate, so the map (and the newly-highlighted hovered country) stays visible through the open dropdown while text remains legible.
v5.0.0 2026 major
  • World map switched to Equal Earth projection — replaces Natural Earth with an equal-area projection, so country sizes on the hub map are proportionally honest rather than distorted, fitting a game that's fundamentally about land area.
  • Hovering the country dropdown highlights it on the map — makes tiny countries like Tuvalu, which are too small to reliably click directly on the map, easy to locate: hover the name in the list and its territory lights up (and is brought to the front so it's visible even under larger neighbours).
v4.16.0 2026 minor
  • 🇲🇦 Morocco added — Arabic/English toggle; two-thirds "agricultural land" is mostly semi-arid rangeland, not cropland. Land use figures exclude the disputed territory of Western Sahara, which is disclosed transparently rather than assumed. The disclaimer covers Morocco's electrostate ambitions: a 52% renewable capacity target by 2030, the Noor Ouarzazate CSP complex, and the UK government's abandonment of the £24bn Xlinks subsea cable project in early 2026 — a setback to Morocco's plans to export clean power abroad. World card: Morocco's exceptional Sahara-adjacent solar resource (~450 ha/TWh, better than Egypt's) means the whole world's electricity could theoretically come from just 31% of Morocco's land — one of the more physically credible "fits" cases in the game.
v4.15.2 2026 patch
  • Fixed scroll fight on submit — v4.15.1's fix to scroll to the land-use graphic was being immediately overridden by a second, later scrollIntoView() call on the world card, fired synchronously within the same click handler. Since both animations were smooth scrolls competing for the same frame, the world card's call always won, undoing the intended fix. Removed the world card's own auto-scroll — it still renders and draws after the usual short delay, but no longer fights for the viewport.
v4.15.1 2026 patch
  • Submit now scrolls to the land-use graphic, not the world card — hitting "Submit all guesses" previously only auto-scrolled once the world card appeared, well below the fold. The proportional area map and score are the actual point of the game; the world card is a bonus feature. All 23 games now scroll to the treemap immediately on submit, letting the world card render further down without demanding attention first.
v4.15.0 2026 minor
  • 🇭🇺 Hungary added — Hungarian/English toggle; 57% farmland, 21.1% forest (a century-long record as of end-2024), and one of the highest solar shares of electricity generation in the world (24% in 2024, over three-quarters of green electricity). The disclaimer notes Hungary's April 2026 election, in which Péter Magyar's Tisza party defeated Viktor Orbán's Fidesz after 16 years in power; the new government has since dropped Hungary's EU veto on Ukraine's accession and moved to restore judicial independence. Energy figures reflect the system inherited from the outgoing government, as the new administration's policy was still forming at time of writing.
  • 🇹🇭 Thailand upgraded to bilingual — added a Thai/English toggle to the previously English-only Thailand game, following the "always add the local language, or explain why not" principle now applied across the game.
v4.14.0 2026 minor
  • 🇹🇭 Thailand added — English only; 41% farmland (a top global rice exporter), 37% forest despite decades of development, and over 250 golf courses making it one of Asia's top golf tourism destinations. Highlights EGAT's floating solar programme on Sirindhorn Dam — the world's largest hydro-floating solar hybrid — which sidesteps land-use conflicts entirely by sharing reservoir surfaces already dammed for hydropower. World card: Thailand's tropical solar resource and reasonably large land area mean the world's electricity could theoretically be powered from just 33% of Thailand's land — one of the more physically grounded "fits within the country" cases in the game.
v4.13.1 2026 patch
  • Tuvalu: EEZ note added — the world card footer now addresses whether Tuvalu's vast 749,790 km² ocean territory (nearly 29,000× its land area) could host offshore solar or wind. Short answer: not easily — open-ocean floating solar has no proven cyclone-resistant design anywhere in the world, and Tuvalu's atolls drop into deep water almost immediately offshore, ruling out fixed-bottom turbines. The EEZ's real value to Tuvalu remains tuna fishing licence fees, not energy.
v4.13.0 2026 minor
  • 🇹🇻 Tuvalu added — English only; one of the four smallest countries on Earth at just 26 km² (2,600 ha), averaging under 2 metres above sea level. Categories highlight the existential threat of sea-level rise for low-lying Pacific nations: pulaka swamp-taro gardens poisoned by saltwater intrusion from below, a freshwater lens contaminated by encroaching seawater, and engineered land built by the Tuvalu Coastal Adaptation Project because there is no higher ground to retreat to. World card: Tuvalu's entire national territory would fit inside the land needed for world solar over 6,400 times — the starkest scale mismatch in the game, underlining just how small and vulnerable this nation of 11,000 people truly is.
v4.12.0 2026 minor
  • 🇳🇬 Nigeria added — English only; Africa's most populous nation (220 million) and largest oil producer, yet generating less grid electricity than Norway. 46% farmland, 22% forest (rapidly declining — one of the world's highest deforestation rates), 25% savanna. Golf courses cover more land than all utility-scale solar farms combined. World card: Nigeria's exceptional solar resources (Sokoto gets 7.5 peak sun hours/day) mean the world's electricity could theoretically be powered from 18% of Nigeria's land — roughly the same area Nigeria already uses for cities and roads.
v4.11.0 2026 minor
  • 🇬🇱 Greenland added — English only; 79% of the world's largest island is buried under the second-largest ice sheet on Earth, reaching 3 km thick. The remaining coastal fringe is tundra, bare rock and sheep pastures. The world card is the most striking in the game: the solar circle technically fits within Greenland's outline at 17.2% of total land — but represents 91% of all ice-free land on the island, essentially the entire exposed coastline. Greenland already generates 87% of its electricity from hydropower (Nukissiorfiit), using glacial meltwater from the retreating ice sheet.
